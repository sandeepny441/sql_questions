Yes, this kit will generally be a bit louder acoustically than a compact mesh‑pad e‑kit, and in an apartment you’ll need to manage both impact noise and shell resonance carefully.[thomannmusic](https://www.thomannmusic.com/efnote_7x_e_drum_set.htm)

## Why it can be louder than typical e‑kits

- The EFNOTE 7X uses full‑size 6‑ply birch wood shells (20" kick, 14" snare, large floor toms), not shallow practice shells, so when you hit them you get more physical resonance from the shells themselves than on small plastic/metal pad frames.[thomannmusic](https://www.thomannmusic.com/efnote_7x_e_drum_set.htm)
    
- The kick drum is a full 20" x 15" shell on the floor; even with a mesh head, the beater impact plus shell vibration transmits low‑frequency thumps through the floor more than a compact pad‑style tower kick, which neighbors below can feel more easily.[thomannmusic](https://www.thomannmusic.com/efnote_7x_e_drum_set.htm)
    
- Larger cymbal pads (16" crash, 20" ride, 14" hi‑hat) spread the impact over more surface and can feel and sound more like real cymbals in the room than smaller 12–13" rubber/mesh pads, adding some extra “chick” and stick noise compared with small, heavily damped pads.[thomannmusic](https://www.thomannmusic.com/efnote_7x_e_drum_set.htm)
    

## Why it is still much quieter than acoustic drums

- All heads are mesh pads, which drastically reduce actual drum volume versus real acoustic heads, so what you get acoustically is mostly stick noise, mesh “swish,” and shell resonance rather than full drum tones.[thomannmusic](https://www.thomannmusic.com/efnote_7x_e_drum_set.htm)
    
- The cymbal and hi‑hat pads are rubber/electronic pads, so they are significantly quieter than actual metal cymbals, and the main loudness factor will be your playing dynamics and how hard you strike the pads.[thomannmusic](https://www.thomannmusic.com/efnote_7x_e_drum_set.htm)
    

## Apartment friendliness in practice

- Compared with a small rack‑mounted e‑kit (e.g., compact Millenium or Roland mesh sets), the EFNOTE 7X is less apartment‑friendly because of its larger shells and heavier kick on the floor, which increase structure‑borne noise and space requirements.[thomannmusic](https://www.thomannmusic.com/efnote_7x_e_drum_set.htm)
    
- However, compared with any full acoustic kit, it’s far more neighbor‑friendly, especially if you add mitigation: thick rug plus isolation pad under the kick, drum rug over foam, and possibly a riser if you have sensitive neighbors below.
    

If your main concern is late‑night practice in a typical apartment with someone directly under you, I’d suggest either a smaller mesh‑head kit or planning a proper isolation platform for the kick and maybe the whole kit; would you be practicing mostly during quiet hours (late night/early morning), or at daytime when some noise is acceptable?
