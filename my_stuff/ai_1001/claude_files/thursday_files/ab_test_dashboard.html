EQ visits (rate checks)

Control: 149,746 → 197,504 (+32%). Treatment: 66,958 → 60,357 (−10%). Unassigned: 736 → 861 (+17%).
Key insights
A 42-point behavioral divergence. In the same 24-day window, Control LOs checked rates 32% more often while Treatment LOs checked 10% less. This is the clearest behavioral fingerprint of the pilot.
Control's surge signals competitive urgency. Rising from 149K to 197K visits — LOs are working harder to find competitive pricing for each loan. This extra effort is converting: submissions and locks both rose.
Treatment's decline is the adoption fingerprint. These LOs handed rate discovery to the dynamic engine — but the engine is not delivering rates competitive enough to convert: submissions fell 25% in the same period.
Net implication: Treatment LOs are doing less rate-checking work yet achieving worse outcomes. The engine may be creating passive dependency while delivering subcompetitive pricing that LOs aren't catching because they've stopped checking manually.

PRO score

Competitive pricing quality metric — higher is better
Before pilot During pilot (Control) During pilot (Treatment) During pilot (Unassigned)
Control: 53.3 → 40.5 (−24%). Treatment: 51.4 → 36.7 (−29%). Unassigned: 20.0 → 27.8 (+39%).
Key insights
Treatment's PRO Score fell 5 points steeper than Control's. Both groups started nearly equal (53.3 vs 51.4), so the divergence is not pre-existing — it's entirely pilot-induced. The dynamic engine is producing less competitive prices.
The matched baseline matters here. Groups were well-paired before the pilot. The widening gap during the pilot period — Control −24% vs. Treatment −29% — is attributable to dynamic pricing adjustments, not LO quality differences.
Unassigned improved +39% — a completely opposite trajectory. Lower-scoring LOs with no pilot exposure improved across the same market window. This makes the pilot groups' declines impossible to attribute to market conditions alone.
Call data corroborates directly: 40% of complaints cited rate competitiveness, with LOs reporting UWM rates 25–50+ bps worse than WF, Chase, and PennyMac — the exact pricing gap that a declining PRO Score reflects.
