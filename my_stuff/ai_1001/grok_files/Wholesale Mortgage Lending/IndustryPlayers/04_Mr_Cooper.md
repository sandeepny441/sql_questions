## Part 1 — Origin & Historical Timeline

### Era 1 — Centex Home Equity (1994–2006)

#### 1994 — Founded as Centex Home Equity
A subsidiary of **Centex Corporation**, one of the largest U.S. homebuilders at the time. Centex Home Equity originated and serviced sub-prime mortgages — the "alt-credit" market that mainstream banks avoided.

#### Late 1990s–early 2000s — Growth in Subprime
Becomes a meaningful sub-prime originator and servicer. Sub-prime is profitable but politically and operationally messy; Centex (the homebuilder parent) doesn't love the optics.

#### 2006 — Sold to Fortress Investment Group
Centex divests its home equity business to **Fortress Investment Group** for **~$575 million**. The business is renamed **Nationstar Mortgage**. This is the seed of what becomes Mr. Cooper.

### Era 2 — Nationstar Mortgage (2006–2017)

#### 2007–2008 — Survives the Financial Crisis
Unlike many subprime lenders that collapse (New Century, IndyMac, Countrywide), Nationstar survives by pivoting from origination to **servicing distressed loans** — the very loans that crashed competitors. Becomes a specialty servicer for non-performing mortgages.

#### 2012 — IPO on NYSE
Nationstar IPOs at $14/share, raising ~$233M. Listed under ticker **NSM**. Fortress retains majority ownership.

#### 2012–2014 — Aggressive MSR Acquisition Spree
As big banks (BofA, Wells, Chase) try to shed servicing rights post-crisis, Nationstar becomes a major acquirer:
- **2012**: Buys Aurora Loan Services (~$63B UPB) from Lehman Brothers estate
- **2013**: Acquires **$215B in MSRs from Bank of America** — transformational deal
- **2013–2014**: Multiple smaller MSR portfolio acquisitions

#### 2014–2016 — Operational Strain
Growth outruns operational maturity. Nationstar accumulates significant **customer service complaints**, regulatory scrutiny (CFPB), and brand damage. Foreclosure mishandling and dual-tracking issues become public.

#### 2017 — Rebrand to "Mr. Cooper"
Nationstar rebrands its consumer-facing servicing brand to **Mr. Cooper** — explicitly to escape the negative associations of "Nationstar." Marketing campaign emphasizes humanization ("Don't talk to a company. Talk to Mr. Cooper.").

The corporate parent remains Nationstar Mortgage Holdings Inc. for now.

### Era 3 — The WMIH Merger and Renaming (2018)

#### 2018 — Acquired by WMIH Corp in a Reverse-Merger-Like Transaction
**WMIH Corp** (NASDAQ: WMIH) acquires Nationstar Mortgage Holdings for **~$3.8 billion** in stock and cash.

**WMIH's value proposition:** WMIH was the shell company that emerged from the bankruptcy of **Washington Mutual (WaMu)** in 2008. The shell carried approximately **$6 billion in net operating loss (NOL) carryforwards** — a tax shield that could offset future income for years.

The transaction structure was designed so the combined entity could utilize the NOLs to dramatically reduce future tax bills. Fortress's interest was largely cashed out.

The new combined entity is renamed **Mr. Cooper Group Inc.** (NASDAQ: COOP) — the consumer brand becomes the corporate name.

### Era 4 — Mr. Cooper Group (2018–Present)

#### 2018 — Acquires Pacific Union Financial
Picks up Pacific Union (origination + servicing) to bolster origination capacity.

#### 2018 — Sagent Carve-Out
Mr. Cooper carves out its servicing technology platform as a separate company — **Sagent** — backed by Warburg Pincus and Fiserv. Mr. Cooper retains a minority stake. The thesis: license servicing tech to peers rather than treat it as proprietary infrastructure.

#### 2020 — Acquires Title365
Acquires title insurance / settlement services business **Title365**. Later sold to Blend in 2021.

#### 2021 — CFPB Settlement
Settles a **CFPB enforcement action** (~$73M) for servicing violations that included failing to properly identify loans entering modification, dual-tracking foreclosure proceedings, and other Reg X violations dating back to the Nationstar era.

#### 2023 Q1 — Acquires Home Point Capital Servicing Portfolio
Acquires **Home Point Capital's** servicing rights — approximately **$83 billion UPB**. Home Point had exited the wholesale channel and was effectively winding down its mortgage operation.

#### 2023 Q3 — Acquires Flagstar Mortgage Operations
Acquires **Flagstar Bank's mortgage business** (servicing + origination) from **New York Community Bancorp** (which had acquired Flagstar in 2022). Adds approximately **$280B UPB in servicing** plus origination capability. Mr. Cooper becomes the **largest non-bank servicer** with > $1 trillion UPB.

#### 2024 — Continued Servicing Growth
Cements position as the dominant non-bank servicer. Wells Fargo continues to sell MSR portfolios; Mr. Cooper is the primary buyer.

#### March 2025 — Rocket Companies Announces Acquisition
**Rocket Companies** (NYSE: RKT) announces an all-stock acquisition of Mr. Cooper at an implied value of **~$9.4 billion**. Mr. Cooper shareholders to receive Rocket stock.

**Strategic rationale (Rocket's perspective):**
- Rocket gains the largest servicing book in the U.S.
- Combined entity will service ~$2.1 trillion in UPB — by far the largest in industry history
- Massive recapture opportunity: Rocket's marketing engine + Mr. Cooper's servicing book = refinance recapture at scale when rates fall
- Combines servicing scale with origination brand power

#### 2025–2026 — Regulatory Review & Integration
Antitrust review, regulatory approvals, and operational integration in process. Deal expected to close 2026.

## Part 2 — Business Model

### The Three Segments

Mr. Cooper operates in three reporting segments, with very different economics:

| Segment | What It Does | % of Profits | Volatility |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Servicing** | Collect payments, manage escrow, handle default on owned and sub-serviced loans | ~85% | Moderate — MSR value swings with rates |
| **Originations** | Originate new loans (refi, purchase) primarily to feed servicing book | ~10% | High — rate cyclical |
| **Xome** | REO marketing, real estate exchange/auction services | ~5% | Cyclical with default cycle |

### How Servicing Makes Money

| Revenue Source | Typical Per Loan |
|---|---|
| Servicing fee strip (25 bp on UPB annually) | ~$625 / year on a $300k loan |
| Float income on escrow balances | ~$50–$100 / loan / year |
| Ancillary fees (late, payoff statements, etc.) | ~$25–$50 / loan / year |
| Sub-servicing fees (where MSR owned elsewhere) | ~$60–$80 / loan / year |
| MSR mark-to-market gains | Variable (rate-driven) |

### The Hedge: Origination vs Servicing

This is the core of the business model:

| Rate Environment | Origination | Servicing (MSR value) |
|---|---|---|
| Rates falling | ↑ (refi wave) | ↓ (prepays accelerate, MSR worth less) |
| Rates rising | ↓ (refi dies) | ↑ (loans stay; MSR worth more) |
| Stable | Steady | Steady |

The two segments **naturally hedge each other**. This is why Mr. Cooper (and other multi-channel firms like Pennymac) are structurally more stable than pure-play originators (like Rocket pre-Mr. Cooper).

### The Recapture Engine

When borrowers in Mr. Cooper's serviced book want to refinance, the company aggressively markets to keep them as customers:

1. Real-time monitoring of servicing book for refi-eligible borrowers
2. Targeted outreach when rates drop or borrower's situation improves
3. Capture borrower in Mr. Cooper origination → retain the MSR
4. If not, MSR transfers to the new servicer (revenue loss)

Recapture rates of 20–30%+ are considered industry-leading. This is one of Mr. Cooper's strategic moats.

### MSR as a Financial Asset

Mortgage Servicing Rights (MSRs) are carried on Mr. Cooper's balance sheet at fair value:

- **MSR portfolio value** typically ranges from 1.0% to 1.5% of UPB
- On $1.3T UPB → MSR carrying value of $13B–$20B
- Marked-to-market each quarter; large GAAP earnings swings driven by rate moves
- Often hedged with **TBA short positions** or **swaptions** to dampen earnings volatility

### Capital Structure

- Investment-grade rated (Moody's, S&P)
- Maintains Ginnie Mae net worth requirements
- Uses MSR-secured financing facilities
- Issues unsecured corporate bonds
- Periodic share repurchases (when stock viewed undervalued)

## Part 3 — What to Know as an Investor

### Key Metrics to Track

| Metric | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **UPB serviced** | Top-line scale driver |
| **MSR fair value** | Largest balance-sheet asset; rate-sensitive |
| **Recapture rate** | Whether servicing book gets monetized via origination |
| **Operating expense per loan** | Operational efficiency; scale economies |
| **Pre-tax income per loan serviced** | Unit economics |
| **Sub-servicing UPB** | Lower-margin but capital-light revenue |
| **Delinquency rate** | Pipeline of default-related costs |
| **Tangible book value per share** | The accounting floor for valuation |

### Earnings Volatility Sources

1. **MSR mark-to-market** — quarterly swings can be hundreds of millions; large GAAP P&L impact.
2. **Hedge gains/losses** — partially offset MSR moves; rarely perfect.
3. **Origination margin compression** — when industry-wide refi volumes spike, margins thin.
4. **Default costs** — counter-cyclical to economy; rise in recessions.
5. **One-time M&A integration costs** — significant in years following acquisitions (Flagstar, Home Point).

### Tax Shield (NOL Legacy)

Mr. Cooper still carries meaningful **NOL carryforwards** from the original WMIH transaction. Effective tax rate has historically been below statutory — a structural earnings boost that gradually depletes.

### Valuation Anchors

Mr. Cooper has historically traded between:
- **0.8x – 1.5x tangible book value** in stable rate periods
- **Below 1.0x book** in stressed periods (rate volatility, credit fears)
- **Above 1.5x book** in favorable cycles (MSR appreciating)

Pre-Rocket-deal announcement (early 2025), trading around 1.2x book.

### Rocket Acquisition Considerations

For investors holding through close:
- **All-stock structure** → exposure becomes Rocket equity
- Synergy potential: massive recapture opportunity
- Integration risk: two large, complex servicing operations
- Regulatory approval risk (DOJ review of mortgage concentration)
- Cultural fit risk (Detroit retail vs Texas servicing operations)

## Part 4 — What to Know as a Mortgage Industry Person

### Why You Encounter Mr. Cooper Constantly

If you originate, broker, or process mortgages, you'll deal with Mr. Cooper in several ways:

#### 1. Servicing Transfer Notifications
Loans you originate routinely transfer to Mr. Cooper. When you sell a closed loan to a correspondent investor or through the agency cash window, the servicing may be released to Mr. Cooper. Borrowers receive a goodbye letter from the previous servicer and a hello letter from Mr. Cooper.

#### 2. Recapture Competition
Mr. Cooper actively markets to its serviced borrowers. If you're trying to refinance a Mr. Cooper-serviced loan, you may find:
- Mr. Cooper has already contacted the borrower with a refi offer
- Borrower is comparing your rate to a Mr. Cooper retention quote
- Mr. Cooper may offer to "match" your quote to retain the customer

#### 3. Payoff Quote Requests
Refinancing or selling a borrower's loan? You'll request a payoff quote from Mr. Cooper. Their portal is operationally functional but historically not loved by brokers/title agents.

#### 4. Servicing Complaints / Escalations
Borrowers sometimes have payment-application, escrow, or default issues that escalate. Mr. Cooper has invested heavily in customer service since the Nationstar era, but volume creates incidents.

### Origination Side: Mr. Cooper Direct & Correspondent

Mr. Cooper's origination footprint is meaningful but smaller than its servicing:
- **Direct-to-consumer** (consumer-direct refi targeted at existing customers)
- **Correspondent** — buys closed loans from smaller lenders
- **Wholesale**: minimal post-Home-Point exit
- **Retail branch**: minimal

The strategic focus is **recapture + correspondent**, not broker wholesale or retail branches.

### Industry-Wide Significance

| Aspect | Why Mr. Cooper matters |
|---|---|
| Largest non-bank servicer | Sets industry expectations for non-bank servicing operations |
| MSR buyer of last resort | Banks selling MSRs almost always include Mr. Cooper as a bidder |
| Sub-servicing scale | Many smaller MSR owners outsource servicing to Mr. Cooper |
| Default management expertise | Specialty area built since 2007 |
| Regulatory benchmark | CFPB and state regulators often shape rules with Mr. Cooper's scale in mind |
| Technology bellwether | Sagent platform partnership signals next-gen servicing direction |
| Post-merger giant | Rocket-Cooper combo will service ~25% of all US residential mortgages |

### The Brand-Versus-Reality Gap

The "Mr. Cooper" rebrand was carefully designed to feel personal and friendly. The underlying business is industrial-scale servicing with default operations, regulatory pressure, and complex MSR accounting. The gap between brand image and operational reality is intentional but worth understanding.

## Part 7 — The Strategic Lesson

Mr. Cooper's story is a case study in three industry truths:

1. **In mortgage, owning servicing > owning origination over the long term.** Servicing is recurring revenue with a balance-sheet asset (MSR). Origination is transactional. Companies that built servicing scale during the 2008–2015 bank-exit window are the structural winners of this era.

2. **Brand can be reset.** Nationstar's reputation was bad enough that a full consumer-brand reset (Mr. Cooper) was worth the cost. Few companies do this successfully; Mr. Cooper did.

3. **Tax structure matters more than people admit.** The WMIH transaction was driven primarily by capturing $6B in NOLs. Sophisticated buyers found a way to use a defunct bankruptcy shell for a strategic mortgage acquisition. Watch the tax-loss-carryforward dynamics in any major mortgage M&A.

The Rocket acquisition, if it closes, will be the largest validation of these truths — and will reshape the U.S. mortgage industry for the next decade.
