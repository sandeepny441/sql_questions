## Bank-Owned Mortgage Operations

Big banks have steadily ceded share to non-bank lenders since 2008. Today they originate primarily through branch channels to existing customers.

| Bank | 2010 share (~) | 2024 share (~) | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wells Fargo | ~24% | ~3% | Sharp decline |
| JPMorgan Chase | ~10% | ~5% | Stable |
| Bank of America | ~17% | ~3% | Sharp decline |
| U.S. Bank | ~5% | ~3% | Stable |
| Citizens | <2% | ~2% | Stable |

Combined bank-channel share has fallen from ~50%+ in 2010 to ~25% in 2024.

## Non-Bank vs Bank Origination Share (Industry-Wide)

| Year | Non-Bank Share | Bank Share |
|---|---|---|
| 2010 | ~30% | ~70% |
| 2015 | ~50% | ~50% |
| 2020 | ~65% | ~35% |
| 2024 | ~75% | ~25% |

The non-bank rise is the most important structural shift in US mortgage origination in 15 years. Drivers: lighter regulation than banks (no CCAR / DFAST), focus on agency loans only, lower operating costs, technology adoption.

## Why Channel Matters

A lender's channel(s) determine: cost structure, customer relationship, regulatory exposure, and pricing strategy.

| Dimension | Retail | Wholesale | Correspondent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Origination cost per loan | ~$11,000 | ~$3,500 (paid to broker) | ~$1,500 |
| Customer relationship | Direct | Via broker | Via smaller lender |
| Hedge / capital markets risk | Held by lender | Held by wholesaler | Buyer holds until sold |
| Volume scalability | Limited by LO headcount | Highly scalable | Most scalable |
| Brand value | High (consumer-facing) | Low (B2B) | Low (B2B) |
| Regulatory complexity | High (consumer-facing) | Medium | Medium |

UWM's dominance demonstrates the **wholesale-only cost advantage** at scale; Rocket's dominance demonstrates the **retail brand advantage** with massive marketing spend. The two models coexist and compete.

## What Determines Volume Ranking (Drivers)

1. **Rate environment** — low rates → refi waves benefit consumer-direct (Rocket, AmeriSave); high rates → purchase market benefits LO-led (UWM, CrossCountry).
2. **Channel preference** — broker-heavy markets (CA, FL, TX) lift wholesale lenders; bank-channel markets (Northeast) lift banks.
3. **Servicing book** — large servicing books fuel recapture (Pennymac, Mr. Cooper).
4. **M&A** — multiple top-20 entries are products of recent rollups.
5. **GSE relationships** — DU/LPA approvals, agency seller status, correspondent commitments.
