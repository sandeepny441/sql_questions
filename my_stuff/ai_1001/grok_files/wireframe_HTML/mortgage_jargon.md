# Mortgage Jargon

**10 Essential Terms for Mortgage Lending**

This reference covers the core vocabulary used by lenders, servicers, and capital markets teams when discussing early payoffs and prepayment behavior.

## 1. EPO (Early Payoff)
A loan that pays off in full within a short window after origination or sale. This is the primary lender pain point when loans leave the portfolio too soon.

## 2. Prepayment
Any principal payment made ahead of the scheduled amortization. Includes both partial extra payments and complete loan payoffs.

## 3. Seasoning Period
The minimum aging period a loan is expected to remain active before an early payoff no longer triggers contractual penalties or economic loss for the lender.

## 4. EPO Window
The contractual look-back period (commonly 90, 120, or 180 days post-closing) during which a payoff is classified as "early" and subject to recapture or fees.

## 5. EPO Recapture
The mechanism by which a lender recovers compensation previously paid to brokers or partners when a loan pays off inside the EPO window.

## 6. SRP Clawback
Recovery of the Service Release Premium (SRP) paid when servicing rights were sold or transferred. Triggered by early payoff events.

## 7. Premium Recapture
The process of reclaiming premium amounts paid by investors for mortgage-backed securities when the underlying loans pay off faster than modeled.

## 8. MSR Runoff
The decline in the value of Mortgage Servicing Rights (MSR) caused by early loan termination and the resulting loss of future servicing cash flows.

## 9. CPR (Conditional Prepayment Rate)
An annualized metric used in MBS analytics to express the speed at which loans in a pool are prepaying. A key input for pricing and risk models.

## 10. Refi Incentive
The economic motivation for a borrower to refinance, usually created when prevailing market interest rates fall meaningfully below the borrower's current note rate.
